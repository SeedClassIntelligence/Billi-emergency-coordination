Why Billi

Built for the moments 911 alone doesn't reach

Calling for help assumes three things go right at once. Each one fails often enough to matter.

📍 That they know where you are

Indoor location handed to dispatch is frequently inaccurate. "Somewhere in this building" costs minutes that a person having a cardiac event does not have.

Billi: location, evidence and context start moving on the trigger itself.

🗣 That you can speak

A child who can't safely reach for a phone. Someone mid-medical-event. A driver with a passenger listening. A call was never a plan that could reach them.

Billi: a spoken safe word, a held button, a detected fall — same response.

🌐 That you'll be understood

A language barrier turns an already-hard call into an impossible one, at the exact moment precision matters most.

Billi: structured context, in writing, to people who already know the plan.

Billi starts from a different assumption: the call shouldn't have to be the trigger. The people it's built for are exactly the people typical emergency response is most likely to miss.

What's in it today

📱 Real alerts on Android

Text alerts sent from the phone's own SIM with the Billi app installed — no third-party gateway in between. Verified on real hardware.

📍 Your phone is the sensor

GPS, microphone, camera and accelerometer — the hardware you already own, working together. Nothing extra to buy.

👥 One account, five people

Priced per account, not per protected person. Work and family on the same seat.

Platform differences and current limits
  • On iPhone and in the browser, alert delivery is limited. Apple permits no app to send a text without a person tapping send, so alerts appear inside the app. Carrier-based delivery is in progress.
  • Watches, BLE tags and smart glasses appear in the interface but aren't connected as live hardware yet. Your phone is the real sensor today.
  • Billi is not a replacement for 911 and is not a professionally monitored alarm service. Billi coordinates the people you choose.
