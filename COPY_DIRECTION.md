# Website Copy Direction

The product moved. The copy didn't. This is the direction to close that gap, section by
section, with proposed replacement lines.

---

## 1. The problem, stated precisely

The submission opens with:

> **Billi is lone-worker protection for the smallest businesses in the economy.**

The website opens with:

> **Safety Coordination Platform**
> Every second accounted for. Every person protected.
> Families · Schools · Businesses · Universities · Hospitals · Communities

These are not the same product. The first has a buyer. The second has a category.

**Naming six audiences names none of them.** A judge opening the live URL under *Small Business
Services* reads "Families · Schools · Universities · Hospitals" and concludes they're looking at
a horizontal safety platform that happens to be entered in the wrong category. Nothing above the
fold tells them otherwise.

The repositioning did land in two places — the pricing section and the six scenarios both speak
to working alone. Everywhere else still speaks to everyone. The result reads like two different
companies sharing a domain.

### Where it's wrong, concretely

| Location | Current | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Hero H1 | "Safety Coordination Platform" | A category, not a promise. Says nothing a competitor couldn't say. |
| Hero line 3 | Six audiences listed | Dilutes to zero. Puts families and hospitals ahead of the actual buyer. |
| Stat cards | "Trigger to GPS Lock", "Escalation Timer" | Engineering specs. A driver doesn't buy a GPS lock time. |
| Features | "👨‍👩‍👧 Family Device Invite" | The single biggest differentiator, filed as a family convenience. |
| Compare → Who It's Built For | Children, autism, elderly, travelers, students, families | **Zero lone workers.** This is the worst mismatch on the site. |

---

## 2. The spine

One sentence the entire site serves:

> **Someone is alone, something goes wrong, and nobody who cares about them knows in time.**

Every section either states that problem, shows Billi solving it, or proves it's real. Anything
that does none of those three is cut.

The second sentence, which is the business argument:

> **Almost everyone facing that problem is a small business — a driver, an aide, a solo agent.
> They *are* the business, and the safety infrastructure a corporate employer buys was never
> available to them.**

---

## 3. Voice rules

**Write to one person.** A rideshare driver at 1 a.m. Not "users", not "families and
organizations". If a line would fit any safety product, it isn't finished.

**Lead with the situation, not the system.** "Alone in a car with a stranger" before "nine
activation paths". The mechanism earns attention only after the reader recognizes themselves.

**Numbers need a consequence.** "45 seconds" is meaningless. "45 seconds before it stops waiting
for one person and widens the circle" is a feature.

**First person is allowed, and it's the strongest asset on the site.** You drove for Uber. You
were bullied. That's standing, not marketing — a founder describing a problem they lived is the
cheapest credibility available and no competitor can copy it.

**Say the ordinary word.** "Someone forces you to cancel it" beats "coercion-resistant
cancellation flow".

### Avoid

- "Platform" as the lead noun — it's what you built, not what they get
- Lists of six audiences
- "Solution", "leverage", "seamless", "empower"
- Spec language with no consequence attached
- Any sentence that would survive a find-and-replace of the product name

## 4. Section-by-section

### Hero — the only section that has to change

**H1** — pick one:

- *"When you work alone, someone should know."*
- *"You're the only one there. You shouldn't be the only one who knows."*
- *"Alone at work isn't the same as safe."*

The first is the strongest: short, plain, and it's a promise rather than a category.

**Subhead** (replaces "Every second accounted for"):

> One trigger sends your location, records what's happening, and tells the people who'd actually
> come for you — whether or not you can reach your phone.

**Third line** (replaces the six-audience list):

> Built for drivers, home health aides, contractors, solo agents — anyone who's the only one
> there. One account covers your family too.

That last clause is doing real work. It's the differentiator and it belongs above the fold.

### Stat cards — same numbers, buyer-facing

| Now | Proposed |
|---|---|
| `< 2 seconds` — Trigger to GPS Lock | `Under 2 seconds` — before anyone knows you triggered it |
| `45 seconds` — Escalation Timer | `45 seconds` — then it stops waiting for one person |
| `9` — Emergency Activation Paths | `9 ways to trigger it` — including without touching your phone |

Consider replacing the third with the business number, since business viability is a judged
criterion and no other card carries it:

> `$14.99/mo` — for up to five people, on a personal card

### How It Works — keep the structure, change the reader

Configure → Detect → Coordinate → Resolve is a sound spine. Rewrite each body to a lone worker:

- **Configure** — "Who comes for you, in what order, and what Billi is allowed to do. Decided
  now, at your kitchen table, not during the emergency."
- **Detect** — "A word you can say out loud without anyone noticing. A button. A crash your
  vehicle reports on its own. All of them reach the same response."
- **Coordinate** — "Everyone you chose sees one live incident, not separate texts they have to
  piece together."
- **Resolve** — "You close it out, and there's a record of exactly what happened."

### Features — one rename, one addition

- **"👨‍👩‍👧 Family Device Invite" → "👥 One account, work and family"**. Body: "Add a second
  device and it gets its own GPS, mic and camera, scoped to whoever it protects. Lone-worker
  tools stop at the worker. Family apps stop at the family. This doesn't."
- **Add "🛡 AI recommends. The rules decide."** — "Gemini reads the live incident and suggests
  actions. A separate rule engine checks each one against the permissions you set, and only then
  approves it. If you withheld audio consent, no model gets to override that."

That second one is the entire AI-Native Operations criterion and it currently appears nowhere in
the features grid.

### Compare page → "Who It's Built For" — the biggest gap

Six personas, none of whom work alone for a living. This is the page a judge opens to check the
category fit, and it currently argues against it. Lead with work, keep family:

1. **Rideshare & delivery drivers** — alone in a car with someone you've never met, on a route
   nobody's watching.
2. **Home health aides** — entering an unfamiliar home alone, several times a day.
3. **Contractors & tradespeople** — a site with nobody else on it.
4. **Real estate agents** — showing an empty property to a stranger who booked online.
5. **Retail & hospitality closing alone** — the last hour of the shift, in an empty building.
6. **Long-haul drivers** — parked overnight, hundreds of miles from anyone who knows you.

Then the existing consumer set — children, elopement risk, elderly wandering — under a heading
that frames it as coverage, not a second market: *"And the same account covers your family."*

### Footer CTA

"Ready to protect what matters?" is filler. Replace with the spine:

> **Nobody should be alone in an emergency because they work alone.**

---

## 5. What not to touch

- **Pricing section** — already reoriented, and the per-account rationale is the best-argued copy
  on the site.
- **The six scenarios** — they carry the positioning correctly.
- **The comparison table** — the row labels are honest and specific.
- **Live status indicators** — `LIVE GEMINI`, `CONNECTED`, `LOCAL MODE`. Not copy; they're the
  product telling the truth at runtime.

---

## 6. One claim to verify before it ships

**RESOLVED 2026-08-13.** The video script and the submission both claimed Billi was "roughly a
tenth of what a commercial lone-worker monitoring contract costs." That number was persuasive and
**uncited**. The submission cites
real figures for Life360, AngelSense, Bay Alarm and ADT — consumer products. It cites nothing for
commercial lone-worker monitoring.

Both were reworded to the version that needs no source, because the procurement point was always
the stronger argument anyway:

> Commercial lone-worker monitoring is sold through procurement. You can't buy it on a personal
> card. That's the reason people who work alone go without it.

That version is unfalsifiable, true, and makes the same point.
